# Gold headlines — Skynet and Chill

Calibration set for kickers and issue subtitles. Every example is verbatim from a published issue (Sep 8 – Oct 26, 2026; 50 issues, 2,248 items). Delimiters are normalized to a colon (or the kicker's own `?`); apostrophes are as published.

## What a "headline" is in this newsletter

Published issues before Nov 2026 had no section headlines; drafts now title each cluster (see "Section titles" below). The item-level editorial unit is the **kicker**: a short joke prefixed to a factual one-sentence summary.

```
[Kicker: Factual summary sentence](url) - _Source_
```

- **Length:** median 3 words; 88% are 5 words or fewer; 7 words is the practical ceiling.
- **Case:** sentence case, except where the referenced title's own capitalization is the joke (`Of Mice and Models`, `A Tale of Two Trillions`).
- **The summary carries the facts; the kicker never replaces them.** The kicker should land on its own and get funnier after reading the summary.
- **Don't repeat the pun word in the summary.** (`Elvis has not left the data set:` → summary starts "Presley estate owner…", not "Elvis Presley estate owner…")

The other two creative slots:
- **Subtitle** (email preheader, line under the H1): 2–8 words riffing on the day's lead theme.
- **Italic aside** under an item: the editor's own voice. Not generated; see house-style.md.

## What makes these work

1. **One reference, instantly recognizable.** Film, song, TV, idiom, meme, or a technical term the reader knows (`rm -rf`, `contango`, `ARR`, `KYC`).
2. **The pun hooks a concrete noun from the story** (Brin, Rubin, Cart, Fission, COBOL → Mainframe). Generic AI wordplay ages fast.
3. **The joke has a point of view about the story** — usually that the powerful party is being absurd, hypocritical or reckless (`Arsonist selling smoke alarms`, `We have investigated ourself, and we’re all good`). Pure wordplay with no angle is weaker.
4. **Short.** Three words is the center of mass. Past six, the kicker competes with the summary.
5. **Target the powerful or absurd party**, never the people harmed. Victims appear in kicker-free items or with a kicker aimed at the company.

## Weaker patterns (from the same issues)

- **Source headline wearing a kicker.** `Subprime AI: The A.I. Boom Looks a Lot Like the Housing Bubble`; `Buy now, cry later: It’s All Fun and Games Until You Give AI Your Credit Card`. The kicker may be fine; the body is the publication's Title Case headline. Rewrite the body as a summary.
- **Label, not joke.** `GPU arbitrage:`, `Hyperbolic:`, `Tested:`. Acceptable as signposts; don't count them as hooks.
- **Kicker needs a fact the summary omits.** `Zuck’s Lula-bye: President says Meta restricts his reach in Brazil election` — Lula is never named.
- **Too long.** `A trillion here, a trillion there, pretty soon you’re talking real money` (13 words); `Facebook, but with autonomy, what could go wrong?` (8). They work, but they're the outer limit.
- **Repeats.** Exact repeats within seven weeks: `Army of one`, `Sweep dreams`, `The Muskening`, `Garbage in, garbage out`. Recycled frames: `Weapons of math construction/destruction`, `Sue-pervised learning` / `Sue-per-intelligence`, `Czar wars` (twice), `The Son also borrows/raises`, `Cloudy with a chance of pitchforks/bankruptcy`, `Silence of the LLMs` (subtitle, then kicker), `Feel the AGI` (kicker, then subtitle), `IPOcalypse Now` (three times). Check the last month before reusing a frame.
